# GRAPHICS-NOTES.md

What this renderer can do beyond TS parity, and what the "grand scene" ambition
needs. Written after the lighting investigation of 2026-07-27/28, whose whole
point was to replace guesses with measurements — every number here came from a
run on this hardware, and every capability claim from reading the vendored
Bevy 0.19 source rather than from memory.

Distribution is moving to this port and away from TS, so deviating from parity
is now allowed where it buys something. D18, D19 and D20 are the precedents.

---

## The measurement that settles the lighting question

Scene: `assets/levels/stress_lights.json` (generated by
`scripts/gen-stress-level.py`) — six galleries stacked around one open atrium,
300 lit sconces, all of them in view of each other. Release build, vsync off,
medians over ~16s each:

| Variant | Frame time |
|---|---|
| 300 lights, distance cull on (pre-D20) | 4.57 ms |
| 300 lights, cull off, forward | 4.63 ms |
| 300 lights, cull off, deferred | 4.54 ms |
| 300 lights, cull off, range 40 (all overlapping), deferred | 4.62 ms |
| 300 lights, cull off, range 40 (all overlapping), forward | 4.62 ms |

Every variant is inside the others' noise. Confirmed by the user in play:
performance is fine, and this scene would not have been possible in the TS
build.

**Conclusions.** Three hundred overlapping shadowless point lights cost nothing
measurable here, so light count is not the constraint on grand scenes.
**Deferred rendering buys nothing** and the port stays on clustered forward —
the switch is one resource (`DefaultOpaqueRendererMethod::deferred()`) if that
ever changes. The reason both hold: roughly 3.5ms of the ~4.6ms frame is
`draw_hud` rasterising a software pixel canvas on the CPU, so lighting is a
small slice of a CPU-bound frame.

The range-40 rows exist because the first runs were nearly meaningless — range-10
lights in a 96-unit cavern barely overlap, so "300 lights" was 300 *independent*
lights. Forcing overlap changed nothing either.

**What this does not prove:** how many lights are simultaneously lighting
*visible* fragments. Some sit behind the camera or outside the frustum, and
lights outside the view cost little. Given every variant landed identically,
including the deliberately-overlapping ones, a finer breakdown seems unlikely to
change the conclusion.

## Why WebGL could not do this

TS's own code documents it. `projectileRenderer.ts`'s `warmUpGPUShaders` says
Three.js bakes `NUM_POINT_LIGHTS` into shader defines, so adding a light at
runtime recompiles every lit material — it pre-warms variants for *ten* extra
lights. A per-fragment loop over all lights, plus a combinatorial shader
problem. Bevy clusters lights spatially and reads them from a storage buffer, so
light count decides which cluster a fragment consults, not what shader gets
compiled.

Relevant limit while reading about this: `MAX_UNIFORM_BUFFER_CLUSTERABLE_OBJECTS`
is 204, but that caps only the *uniform-buffer fallback* path for platforms
without three storage-buffer bindings (WebGL2). Native desktop uses storage
buffers, where the limits are 16384 cluster indices and up to 511 lights
touching a single cluster.

## What Bevy 0.19 ships (module-level, verified present)

`bevy_pbr`: clustered forward, deferred, prepass, SSAO, screen-space
reflections, volumetric fog, atmosphere, decals, contact shadows, environment
maps, light probes, lightmaps, area lights via LTC, transmission, parallax
mapping, meshlets. `bevy_post_process`: bloom, depth of field, motion blur, auto
exposure. `bevy_anti_alias`: TAA, SMAA, FXAA, CAS, DLSS.

Presence is confirmed; behaviour on macOS/Metal is not. Some (DLSS, meshlets)
carry hardware or feature-flag constraints to check before relying on them.

## The actual blockers for grand scenes

**No night-cavern fog preset.** This is the first thing to author. Fog is per
environment and hardcoded in `environment.rs`:

| Environment | fog near → far | colour |
|---|---|---|
| Dungeon | 6 → 26 | black |
| Mist | 2 → 14 | grey |
| Forest | 4 → 20 | dark green |
| Outdoor | 20 → 80 | daylight blue |

Nothing has both a dark colour and a long view. Dungeon fog goes fully black at
26 world units, less than the width of `stress_lights`' atrium, which is why
that scene ships set to `outdoor` — the only preset that can see across itself,
at the cost of reading as an overcast canyon rather than a lit hall. A preset
with dark fog and a far distance is roughly ten lines, and `MAX_FOG_FAR` must
rise with it (see D20).

**Billboard lighting scales with light count.** `billboard::apply_neutral_lighting`
sums every light per sprite on the CPU. Fine today and linear — thirty sprites
against three hundred lights is microseconds — but it is also why forest trees
are still ordinary lit surfaces and still tint under coloured light: they share
one material per variant, and brightness is written into the material. A real
GPU shader for billboards fixes the forest and removes the scaling concern at
once. This is the one piece of the lighting path that genuinely wants rewriting.

**Volumetric fog with sprites is untested here.** It should composite against
scene depth, and the sprites are alpha-masked so they write depth — but sprites
will not scatter light themselves, so a torch behind a tree gets no shaft.
Expected, not verified.

## Baked lightmaps, and how to flicker them

Lightmaps are the strongest answer for a *static* cavern: hundreds of lights
baked to a texture, no runtime light cost, and they compose with real lights on
top since Bevy lightmaps supply indirect diffuse that adds to real-time
lighting.

The catch found while checking: **Bevy's `Lightmap` component has no intensity
field** — only `image`, `uv_rect`, `bicubic_sampling`. A baked lightmap cannot
be dimmed or pulsed through the component API. Three ways around it:

1. **Hybrid, cheapest and probably right.** Bake the static hundreds; leave the
   few flickering lights (player torch, sconces) as real dynamic lights. No
   shader work at all.
2. **Light styles, the Quake trick.** Bake a separate lightmap layer per
   flickering group and blend them by per-frame scalars — Quake's
   `"mmnmmommommnonmmonqnmmo"` strings were literally per-frame brightness
   sequences. Needs an `ExtendedMaterial` that samples base plus flicker layers;
   that mechanism is already used in this codebase for the depth-ignoring damage
   numbers, so the path is known-viable.
3. **`uv_rect` atlas swap.** Pack baked variants into one atlas and move
   `uv_rect` per frame. No shader, but stepped rather than smooth, and it
   multiplies lightmap memory.

Sampling the baked lightmap under a sprite's cell — instead of summing lights
per sprite — is a good idea worth pursuing alongside: cheaper, stable, and
automatically consistent with the floor the sprite stands on. It needs the
lightmap readable where brightness is computed, which is another argument for
moving billboard lighting into a shader.

## Cheap visual wins, roughly in order of payoff per effort

Bloom on sconce flames and emissive materials (hundreds of glowing embers or
crystals cost nothing in lighting), a night-cavern fog preset, SSAO for corner
contact shadows, SMAA or TAA for edges, volumetric fog with a few lights for
shafts. Each is close to a component insert and independently revertible.
